A place to ask “what happens if…?” Choose a cube from three to six dimensions, adjust its rotation controls, and watch the projection respond.

Start small. Add another plane.

Use the app’s dimension selector and rotation controls to isolate a motion. Compare it with a combination. Return a rate to zero when you want to remove its contribution.

In 3D, an axis gives us a familiar way to name rotation. Higher-dimensional motion is easier to explain in terms of planes: two coordinate directions that turn into one another.

Appearance controls let you hide the edges or choose another color. Corners alone can reveal patterns that a dense web of edges obscures.

Let one vertex tell the story.

The white corner is a vertex. Its gold trail turns a fleeting movement into a visible path. Use the controls below to compare a single-plane rotation with two combined rotations.

Enable JavaScript to try tracing. A single-plane orbit is circular in its own plane; its projection can look like an ellipse or a line.

Educational browser illustration, using coordinate-plane step rotations and the app’s dimensional reduction. It is not the Android app running in the browser. In 5D and 6D, these presets rotate the first four coordinates; the extra coordinates stay fixed.

One plane

Choose 4D and “One plane · XW.” A corner follows a circle in its rotation plane. On screen, that orbit can flatten into a line.

Two planes

Compare “XW + YW” with “XY + ZW.” The first pair shares W. The second pair acts on separate coordinate directions, so it can produce richer paths in 4D.

Pause to inspect

Pause holds the cube and trail in place. Resume continues. Restart trace clears the history while keeping the current orientation.

In the Android app: Open the appearance controls to enable Trace vertex, adjust Trail duration from 1–60 seconds, or choose Restart trace. The default duration is 10 seconds. Use Pause/Resume to inspect the motion in Sandbox and Muse, with or without tracing enabled. The trail is a rolling history; crossings and returns do not freeze it automatically.

What a trail can tell you

A trace shows where the corner has recently been, after projection. Repeated loops can reveal regularity. A crossing on screen can also hide two points with different unseen coordinates.

Some combinations repeat. Others have no finite common cycle. Trail length is measured in seconds so the control remains meaningful in either case.
